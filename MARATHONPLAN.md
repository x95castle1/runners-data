## Fuelling

You will burn roughly **4,150 calories** — your 22-miler ran 158 cal/mile.
Nobody replaces that. The aim is to keep blood glucose up and delay the wall,
which means **30–60 g of carbohydrate an hour**, not calorie matching.

### The schedule

**One GU every 3 miles, starting at mile 3.** At 13:10/mi that is a gel roughly
every 40 minutes.

| Gel | Where | Elapsed |
|---|---|---|
| — | 15 min before the gun | — |
| 1 | mile 3 | 0:39 |
| 2 | mile 6 | 1:19 |
| 3 | mile 9 | 1:58 |
| 4 | mile 12 | 2:38 |
| 5 | mile 15 | 3:17 |
| 6 | mile 18 | 3:57 |
| 7 | mile 21 | 4:36 |
| 8 | mile 24 | 5:16 |

**9 gels = 198 g carbohydrate, 900 calories, 1,125 mg sodium** — about 34 g of
carbohydrate an hour from gels, landing in the 45–60 g range once on-course
sports drink is added.

**Carry 10.** One will be dropped, and one aid station will be a scrum.

### Rules

- **Always with water, never with sports drink.** A gel plus a sugary drink is
  too concentrated for the gut to empty, and that is how people end up in the
  portaloo at mile 19. Take water at the station where the gel goes down.
- **Take it just before an aid station**, not just after.
- **Caffeine late.** Non-caffeinated for the first four, caffeinated from mile 15
  if that is what you have trained with. It keeps the lift for when it matters
  and keeps the total moderate.
- **Set a watch alarm every 40 minutes.** At mile 20 you will not be doing
  arithmetic.

### Fluid and sodium

In the seventies over five-plus hours, fluid matters more than the gels do.

- **16–24 oz an hour.** Take something at nearly every aid station rather than
  large amounts occasionally.
- **Sodium:** gels give 1,125 mg; a hot five-hour effort can cost several times
  that. Use the on-course electrolyte drink between gels, or carry salt capsules
  **if you have already trained with them**.
- Early thirst in a hot race is a real signal. Drink to it.

### Race morning

- **3 hours before:** 600–800 cal, familiar, low fibre and low fat. Whatever you
  ate before the 22-miler.
- **Then sip water** — roughly 16 oz over the next two hours; stop 30 min before.
- **15 min before the gun:** one gel with water.

## Nothing new on Sunday

Six days out is far too late to introduce a gel flavour, a salt tab, or a
breakfast you have not run on. Everything above assumes you use what your gut
already knows. Where it conflicts with something that has worked for you in
training, **training wins**.

This is analysis of your own training data plus general endurance-fuelling
guidance. It is not coaching and not medical advice — it knows nothing about how
you feel, your GI tolerance, or anything a doctor would want to weigh in on.

---

## If the day goes sideways

- **Hot and struggling by mile 16:** give back 20–30 sec/mile and keep taking
  gels. Finishing at 6:05 is a far better day than walking from 20.
- **Nauseous:** stop the gels for one cycle, keep drinking water, try again at
  the next. Forcing a gel onto a closed stomach ends the race.
- **Flying at mile 20:** the plan already allows for it. That is what the easy
  first six miles bought you.

Good luck. The 22-miler says you know how to run this.
